Oak facts
  1. Oaks support more forms of life than any other tree genus in North America
  2. Masting is a phenomenon when oaks produce an outsized crop of acorns.
  3. Marcescence is the retention of dead leaves on the tree well after other trees are completely bare, common in the Fagaceae, the tree family that includes oaks, beeches and chestnuts.
Oaks and biodiversity

Most song birds in North America are insectivores, particularly during the nesting period, with seeds and berries only supplementing their diet. All insects are produced by plants — they either eat some part of plant or they eat some other animal that ate some plant part. When we reduce the amount of plants, we reduce the diversity and abundance of insects. We have removed more than half of the forests on Earth and insect populations have declined globally by at least 45% since 1979 (Dirzo et al. 2014). As a result, there are now 3 billion fewer birds in North America than there were just 50 years ago (Rosenberg et al. 2019), and over 430 bird species in North America are declining so rapidly that they are now considered at risk of extinction (2016 State of Birds Report).

Plants are necessary to produce insects but not all plants produce equal number of insects. The ability to produce insects depends on whether the plant is native and on the differences between native plants themselves.

Because plants lace their leaves with nasty or toxic chemicals to keep herbivores from eating them, insects had to adapt chemical defenses physiologically and behaviorally. Most insects have become very good at eating a few plants but are completely unable to eat most plants — this is called “host plant specialization.”

About 75% of the insect food required by birds and other animals is produced by just a few plant genera. In most US counties, oaks, cherries, willows, birches, hickories, pines and maples are producing the vast numbers and types of insects that support animal populations — these are keystone plants.

No other tree genus supports as much life as oaks. The number of species of caterpillars is a good proxy measure for how much life a tree can support.
